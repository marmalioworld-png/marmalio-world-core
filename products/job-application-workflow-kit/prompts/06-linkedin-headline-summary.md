# Prompt 06 — LinkedIn Headline & Summary

## Purpose

Bring your LinkedIn headline and About section in line with the same, truthful positioning as your resume and cover letter — consistent, not duplicated word-for-word.

## Required Inputs

- Your Experience Inventory (Prompt 02 output).
- The target roles or industries you want your profile to attract (can be more general than one specific posting).

## The Prompt

```
You are rewriting my LinkedIn headline and About section. Use only the experience in the inventory below — do not add any employer, skill, achievement, or outcome that is not in it.

My experience inventory:
"""
[PASTE PROMPT 02 OUTPUT HERE]
"""

Target roles/industries I want to attract: [DESCRIBE, e.g., "Operations management roles in mid-size retail or logistics companies"]

Produce:
1. THREE headline options (under 220 characters each), each taking a slightly different angle (e.g., one skills-forward, one outcome-forward, one role-forward) — all grounded in the inventory.
2. ONE About section draft (150–300 words), written in first person, that summarizes my real background and what I'm looking for next, without restating my resume bullet-by-bullet.

Do not use generic filler phrases ("results-driven professional," "passionate about excellence") unless they are directly supported by something specific in the inventory. Do not claim expertise in a tool or skill that isn't in the inventory.
```

## Expected Output

Three headline options and one About section draft, each consistent with (but not copy-pasted from) your resume and cover letter, using only inventory-backed claims.

## Truth Check

Check the About section for generic phrases that aren't backed by a specific inventory detail — these are the most common place vague overclaiming sneaks in on LinkedIn specifically.

## Refinement Step

```
Go through the About section sentence by sentence. Flag any sentence that is generic filler not tied to a specific detail from my inventory, and either replace it with an inventory-backed sentence or remove it.
```
