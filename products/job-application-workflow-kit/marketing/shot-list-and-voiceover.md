# Shot List & Voiceover Text — for InVideo AI and ElevenLabs Production

This file turns the 6 scripts in `video-scripts-6.md` into production-ready instructions. Nothing here has been rendered yet — this is the input for the InVideo AI (visual assembly) and ElevenLabs (voiceover) production step, which has not started.

**Voice direction (all scripts):** calm, direct, slightly conversational — not hyped or salesy. Matches the product's honest positioning. Pace: moderate, with a brief pause after the hook line.

---

## Script 1 — "Your resume isn't the problem"

**Shot list (faceless, text-card + screen-recording style):**
1. (0–3s) Full-screen text card, bold: "Your resume isn't the problem. Your prompt is."
2. (3–12s) Screen-recording style mockup of a generic ChatGPT reply full of buzzwords, with "results-driven professional" highlighted/circled.
3. (12–20s) Cut to text card: "That's not a resume problem. That's a PROCESS problem."
4. (20–30s) Quick text-card sequence naming the 9-step system (fast cuts, ~1s each).
5. (30–40s) End card: product name, price, "link in bio."

**Voiceover script (for ElevenLabs):**
> "Your resume isn't the problem. Your prompt is. [pause] Most people ask ChatGPT to improve their resume, and get back buzzwords like 'results-driven' and 'proven track record' — words that could describe literally anyone. That's not a resume problem. That's a process problem. You need to give the AI your real experience first, in detail, before it tailors anything. One prompt can't do that. A system can. I built a nine-step system that does this properly. Link in bio."

## Script 2 — Before/after demo

**Shot list:**
1. (0–3s) Text card: "Watch this resume bullet before and after."
2. (3–15s) Split-screen or sequential text card, red label "BEFORE": the generic bullet, full text on screen long enough to read.
3. (15–28s) Same layout, green label "AFTER": the specific bullet.
4. (28–35s) Text card: "Same facts. Just honest and specific."
5. (35–40s) End card.

**Voiceover script:**
> "Watch this resume bullet, before and after. Before: 'Spearheaded a transformative initiative that dramatically improved operational efficiency.' Sounds impressive. Means nothing. After — using the exact same facts: 'Led company-wide P.O.S. rollout, training twenty-two staff in two weeks.' Same person, same facts. Just specific instead of vague. That's the entire difference this system makes."

## Script 3 — "3 things your resume prompt is missing"

**Shot list:**
1. (0–3s) Text card: "3 things your 'ChatGPT resume prompt' is missing."
2. (3–13s) Numbered card "1. Real experience captured first" with a simple icon/graphic.
3. (13–23s) Numbered card "2. A truth check."
4. (23–33s) Numbered card "3. Tailored to THIS posting."
5. (33–40s) End card.

**Voiceover script:**
> "Three things your ChatGPT resume prompt is missing. One: a step that actually captures your real experience before anything gets written. Two: a truth check, so the AI can't quietly invent a number you never gave it. Three: tailoring to the specific posting, not a generic version of your job title. Most one-off prompts skip all three. All three, built in. Link in bio."

## Script 4 — Myth-busting

**Shot list:**
1. (0–3s) Text card, red X icon: "No, AI can't guarantee you an interview."
2. (3–15s) Text card continuing: "Anyone who says otherwise is lying to you."
3. (15–30s) Text card, green check icon: "What it CAN do: honest, clear, tailored materials."
4. (30–40s) End card.

**Voiceover script:**
> "No, AI can't guarantee you an interview. Here's what it actually can do. Anyone promising guaranteed interviews from a resume tool is lying to you — no document controls what an employer decides. What a good process can do is make sure your real experience is clearly, honestly, and specifically presented for the exact role you're applying to. That's a real, achievable thing. That's what this is."

## Script 5 — Behind-the-scenes

**Shot list:**
1. (0–3s) Text card: "Here's exactly what's inside this system."
2. (3–30s) Fast sequential text cards, one per prompt/step name (~2.5s each): Job Posting Analysis → Experience Inventory → Resume Bullets → Resume Tailoring → Cover Letter → LinkedIn → Interview Prep → Salary Negotiation → Career Change.
3. (30–40s) End card: "Plus templates + a truth checklist. Full breakdown on the product page."

**Voiceover script:**
> "Here's exactly what's inside. Nine prompts, in order: job posting analysis, experience inventory, resume bullets, tailoring, cover letter, LinkedIn, interview prep, salary negotiation, career change. Two resume templates. A cover letter template. And a truth checklist you run before you send anything — so nothing gets invented along the way."

## Script 6 — Career-changer angle (LinkedIn cut)

**Shot list:** more text-forward, fewer cuts, suited to LinkedIn's native video style.
1. (0–4s) Text card: "Changing careers? Don't let your resume imply experience you don't have."
2. (4–25s) Three-column text card building up one column at a time: "Directly transfers" / "Partially transfers" / "Real gap."
3. (25–35s) Text card: "Honest about the transition. Not pretending it isn't one."
4. (35–40s) End card.

**Voiceover script:**
> "Changing careers? Don't let your resume imply experience you don't have. The hardest part of a career change isn't hiding the gap — it's being honest about it without underselling what actually transfers. This system has a dedicated career-change workflow. It separates what directly transfers, what partially transfers, and what's a real gap — then helps you write a narrative that's honest about the transition instead of pretending it isn't one."

## Production notes

- All six use the same end-card template (see `canva-graphic-briefs.md`) for brand consistency.
- No stock footage of people is required or used — this stays fully faceless per the brief.
- Captions should be burned in (auto-caption via InVideo AI) since a large share of social viewing is sound-off.

## Localization readiness

The shot lists in this file (visual structure, pacing, cut timing) are locale-independent and reusable as-is for every future language version. Only two things change per locale: the voiceover text (re-recorded in ElevenLabs using a native or native-sounding voice for the target language, not a translated script read by the English voice) and the on-screen text cards (re-set in the target language, not machine-translated in place — see `localization/localization-vs-translation.md` for why the video hooks specifically need adaptation, not literal translation, since a hook that lands in English rarely lands the same way translated word-for-word).
