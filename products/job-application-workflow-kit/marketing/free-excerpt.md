## Why your AI-written resume sounds like everyone else's

If you've asked ChatGPT to "improve my resume bullet," you've probably gotten something like this back:

> Spearheaded a transformative initiative that dramatically improved operational efficiency.

It sounds impressive. It also says almost nothing. Swap out the noun and it could describe a hundred different jobs — which is exactly the problem: generic-sounding AI output doesn't help you stand out, because it isn't actually specific to you.

The fix isn't a better prompt. It's a better *process* — one that starts by getting your real, specific experience down first, and only tailors and polishes after that, never inventing along the way. ## Free prompt: Job Posting Analysis

Before you touch your resume, you need to know exactly what the role is actually asking for — not what you assume it's asking for.

**Required input:** the full text of one job posting you're applying to.

**The prompt:**

```
You are helping me analyze a job posting before I tailor my application materials to it.

Use only the information explicitly provided in this conversation. Do not invent, assume, or infer any requirement, responsibility, or expectation that is not stated in the posting text below. If something in the posting is vague or ambiguous, note it as unclear rather than resolving it yourself.

Job posting text:
"""
[PASTE FULL JOB POSTING HERE]
"""

Produce a structured breakdown with these sections:

1. MUST-HAVE REQUIREMENTS — explicitly required qualifications, experience, or skills.
2. NICE-TO-HAVE / PREFERRED — explicitly stated as preferred, bonus, or a plus.
3. RECURRING KEYWORDS AND THEMES — words, phrases, or concepts that appear more than once or are clearly emphasized.
4. IMPLIED PRIORITIES — what the posting's structure, order, or emphasis suggests matters most to this employer (label this section as inference).
5. UNCLEAR OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS — anything vague, contradictory, or open to more than one interpretation.

Do not evaluate whether I am a good fit. That is not this step's job.
```

Try it on a real posting you're interested in. You'll immediately have a clearer, more specific picture of the role than most applicants bother to build — and a head start on tailoring everything else.
